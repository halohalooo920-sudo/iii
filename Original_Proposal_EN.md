# Original Proposal — Group Guidance Programme

## Self-Exploration and Career Direction for Young Adults Navigating Uncertainty

*Online personal-growth group for emerging adults feeling lost about their future direction*

*Submitted for supervisor approval — May 2026*

---

## 1. What is the rationale for designing the group guidance programme?

Many Chinese young people in the emerging-adulthood stage (Arnett, 2000) feel lost about their future career and life direction. The reasons for being stuck vary from person to person: some lack self-exploration experience; some are overwhelmed by parental or societal expectations; some have stopped exploring under the weight of uncertainty; and some fear the consequences of making a choice.

Career Construction Theory (CCT; Savickas, 2005, 2013) offers a lens for understanding these difficulties. CCT holds that individuals actively construct their careers through three interpretive and interpersonal processes — organizing themselves, imposing direction on their vocational behaviour, and making meaning of their careers — and that completing these tasks requires Career Adaptability resources: Concern, Control, Curiosity, and Confidence (Savickas & Porfeli, 2012). 

CCT is chosen as the primary framework because these young people's core difficulty is not "which job fits me" — a trait-factor matching question that traditional vocational guidance addresses — but rather "I cannot figure out who I am or where I am going," which is exactly the self-construction process CCT is designed for. A trait-factor or information-giving approach would miss the mark; what is needed is a process that helps them build self-knowledge, derive direction from that knowledge, and make personal meaning of the journey.

The programme draws on several complementary theories and tools. Amundson's (2018) Active Engagement model supplies a dynamic, non-linear process principle — sessions have structure but follow the members' energy rather than a rigid script. Rosenberg's (2015) Nonviolent Communication (NVC) equips members in the later sessions to express their emerging self-understanding to significant others (particularly parents) without escalating conflict. Rogers' (1957) Person-Centered core conditions — unconditional positive regard, congruence, and empathic understanding — ground the group's safe atmosphere, ensuring members feel free to be uncertain and contradictory without judgement. Holland's (1997) RIASEC typology is used not as a psychometric test but as a vocabulary tool to help members articulate interests they may never have named.

Meta-analytic evidence supports the viability of this design: Rudolph et al. (2017) confirm that Career Adaptability can be effectively enhanced through structured group intervention, providing confidence that a well-designed 18-hour programme can produce meaningful change in the four adaptability dimensions targeted here.

## 2. Purpose: What are the goals and objectives?

**Overall goal**

To facilitate participants' career construction process and enhance their Career Adaptability — particularly Control, Curiosity, and Confidence.

**Specific objectives**

By the end of the nine sessions, participants will be able to:

1. Articulate their interests, strengths, and values.
2. Integrate their self-knowledge to identify at least one possible direction.
3. Identify barriers that obstruct their career construction and supports available to them.
4. Practise expressing their thoughts and needs to a significant other using the NVC framework.
5. Commit to at least one concrete, values-aligned action step.

---

## 3. Describe briefly the contents and activities.

The programme consists of nine group sessions unfolding along the CCT career construction process.

The opening session establishes group safety and norms, conducts a needs check-in, and activates Career Concern. The next sessions move into inward exploration, focusing in turn on interests (e.g., RIASEC discussion and flow-moment recall), strengths and skills (e.g., a skills inventory and a "how friends see me" peer-feedback exercise), and values (e.g., a Values Card Sort).

The middle session integrates the accumulated discoveries to identify possible directions and addresses both internal and external barriers along with the supports available to each member. The later sessions introduce the NVC four-component model and use role-play to practise expressing oneself to significant others, then help members develop a concrete, values-aligned small action step. The final session reviews the entire Miro canvas, includes writing a letter to one's future self, and concludes with a brief closing ceremony, completing the meaning-making process.

Throughout the nine sessions, a shared Miro collaborative canvas serves as a visual tool. Each member gradually builds their own "self-exploration map," which itself becomes the visible product of career construction. While each session has a primary exploration direction, discussion is not strictly confined to a single theme; members are encouraged to move fluidly across elements, consistent with Amundson's Active Engagement principle.

---

## 4. How many sessions? Timeline?

- **Sessions:** 9 group sessions × 2 hours = 18 hours total.
- **Frequency:** 2–3 times per week.
- **Timeline:** Early to late June 2026 (approximately 3–4 weeks).
- **Format:** Online via Zoom plus a Miro collaborative canvas.
- **Individual counselling:** 1 hour per member (minimum 4 hours total), scheduled after Sessions 5–6.
- The detailed schedule will be finalised based on members' availability after recruitment.

---

## 5. How will you ensure objectives are measurable and attainable?

**Measurability**

Change in Career Adaptability is measured by administering the Career Adapt-Abilities Scale (CAAS; Savickas & Porfeli, 2012) before and after the programme. Change in self-clarity is captured through self-designed single-item scaling questions (e.g., "How well do you know your interests / values / strengths, on a 1–10 scale?") administered pre and post. Because some objectives — such as self-clarity — lack a corresponding standardised scale, these brief self-report items serve as a practical complement; such short scaling tools are widely used in evaluating short-term group counselling outcomes (Corey et al., 2018). Achievement of the specific objectives is assessed qualitatively through each member's verbal report in Session 9 and the visible product of their Miro canvas. NVC skill development is observed during the Session 7 role-play and through members' self-assessment. Overall satisfaction is gathered via a post-programme feedback questionnaire combining Likert items with open-ended questions. Finally, the facilitator keeps observation notes after each session to track process-level changes throughout the programme.

**Attainability**

The programme uses a progressive design that follows CCT's natural sequence — organize, then impose direction, then make meaning — so that self-knowledge is built before members are asked to look outward toward direction and action. The objectives are deliberately modest: members are not expected to make a final career decision by the end, but rather to know themselves better, see at least one possible direction, and commit to one small step. The content is also not rigid. Real members' needs are surfaced through the open questions in the screening questionnaire and the needs check-in in Session 1. If the predesigned content does not match the actual needs of the group, later sessions will be adjusted so that the objectives remain attainable for the specific people in the room.

---

## 6. Evaluation strategies?

**Formative (process) evaluation**

- Brief check-in and check-out at every session.
- Tracking the progression of each member's Miro canvas across sessions.
- A mid-programme feedback round after Session 5.
- Facilitator's post-session observation notes.

**Summative (outcome) evaluation**

- Pre/post measures: CAAS plus the self-designed self-clarity scaling questions.
- Post-programme feedback questionnaire (Likert items plus open questions).
- Each member's reflection and action plan presented in Session 9.
- Individual progress noted during the individual counselling sessions.

---

## 7. Recruitment and selection procedures?

**Recruitment**

- Members are recruited through the facilitator's personal network: alumni connections, less-close classmates, friends-of-friends, and similar contacts.
- Recruitment messages describe the topic in everyday language, for example: "Not sure what to do after graduation? Feeling lost about your career or life direction? Stuck between further study and starting work, between staying or going back home? If you would like to explore yourself in a safe small group, here is some information."
- To avoid dual relationships, the facilitator does not recruit close friends, roommates, or anyone with a power or interest relationship to the facilitator.

**Selection**

- Interested individuals complete a short online screening questionnaire covering:
  - Basic information (age, current life stage).
  - Open question: "Please briefly describe how you currently feel about your future career or life direction."
  - Open question: "What do you most hope to gain from this group?"
  - Exclusion criteria screening (current psychiatric treatment; current severe psychological crisis).
- A 10–15 minute online individual pre-meeting confirms fit, explains the group's structure and norms, and answers questions.
- Target group size is 6 members. If more than 6 suitable applicants apply, selection is made by experience diversity and level of commitment.

---

## 8. How are the members going to be prepared for the group experience?

**Pre-group preparation**

1. **Pre-test (baseline data):** Members complete the CAAS scale plus the self-designed self-clarity scaling questions before the programme starts.
2. **Informed consent:** Members sign a consent form covering the group's purpose and structure, confidentiality and its limits, recording policy, and the right to withdraw.
3. **Pre-group individual conversation (10–15 minutes):**
   - Clarify expectations about what the group is and is not.
   - Respond to any concerns or anxiety.
   - Build initial trust with the facilitator.
4. **Information sheet:** A one-page document covering the schedule, the Zoom link, brief Miro instructions, and the group rules.

**Preparation activities within Session 1**

- Icebreaker and self-introduction to reduce anxiety.
- Co-construction of group norms (confidentiality, respect, voluntary participation, the right to pass).
- Explicit discussion of confidentiality and its limits.
- Needs check-in: "Why are you here? What do you hope to take away from this group?"
- Concern assessment and activation: "What thinking or attempts have you already made about your future direction up to this point?"
