## 1. What is the rationale for designing the group guidance programme?

Many Chinese young people in the emerging-adulthood stage (Arnett, 2000) feel lost about their future career and life direction. Literature links this to parental overinvolvement and psychological control (Wang, 2023; Zhao & Li, 2022; Wei et al., 2022) compounded by broader social uncertainty (Yu et al., 2026).

When examined more closely, their difficulties share a common pattern. They have not yet developed a clear sense of who they are, with interests, values, and strengths remaining unclear. They cannot translate whatever self-knowledge they do have into a direction. And they tend to see their past experiences as fragmented rather than forming any coherent story. In Career Construction Theory terms (Savickas, 2005, 2013), this maps onto a breakdown across the three tasks of career construction (organizing the self, imposing direction, and making meaning), with the adaptability resources needed to carry them out, namely Concern, Control, Curiosity, and Confidence (Savickas & Porfeli, 2012), notably weakened.

This is why CCT serves as the primary framework for this programme. It does not simply offer career information or match personality to jobs, but provides a structured process for building the self-knowledge that is currently missing, deriving direction from it, and weaving meaning along the way. In practice, this process works through Amundson's (2018) Active Engagement model, which allows sessions to follow members' energy rather than a fixed sequence. Meta-analytic evidence (Rudolph et al., 2017) confirms that career adaptability can be effectively enhanced through structured group intervention.

## 2. Purpose: What are the goals and objectives of the guidance programme?

**Overall goal:** To facilitate participants' career construction process and enhance their career adaptability.

**Specific objectives:** By the end of the programme, participants will be able to

1. Articulate their interests, strengths, and values.
2. Integrate their self-knowledge to identify at least one possible direction.
3. Identify barriers that obstruct their career construction and supports available to them.
4. Practise expressing their thoughts and needs to a significant other using the NVC framework (Rosenberg, 2015).
5. Commit to at least one concrete action step.

---

## 3. Describe briefly the contents and activities of the group guidance programme.

The programme consists of nine group sessions structured around the three career construction tasks described by Savickas (2005, 2013).

In the organize phase (Sessions 1 through 4), members build self-knowledge through structured exploration of interests (for example, RIASEC discussion and flow-moment recall), strengths (for example, peer feedback and skills inventory), and values (for example, Values Card Sort with source-auditing). These sessions primarily target Concern and Curiosity.

In the impose direction phase (Sessions 5 through 8), members integrate their discoveries to identify possible directions, analyse internal and external barriers, practise expressing their needs to significant others using NVC role-play (Rosenberg, 2015), and commit to a concrete, values-aligned action step. These sessions develop Control and Confidence.

In the make meaning phase (Session 9), members review their full Career Construction Map, write a letter to their future selves, and close with a brief ceremony, integrating the journey into a coherent personal narrative.

Throughout, a shared Miro collaborative canvas accumulates each member's exploration visually, and discussion is not confined to a single theme per session, consistent with Amundson's (2018) Active Engagement principle.

## 5. How will you ensure that the objectives are measurable and attainable within the specified timeline?

Each objective has a visible indicator of achievement within the sessions. Self-knowledge articulation is evidenced by interests patterns and a strengths list on each member's Miro canvas. Communication skills will be observed in the NVC role-play performance in Session 7. Committed action includes publicly stating a specific step in Session 8.

These objectives are attainable for three reasons. First, the progressive design follows CCT's natural sequence so that self-knowledge is built before direction and action are attempted. Second, the objectives are modest. Participants are not expected to make a final career decision but rather to know themselves better, see a direction, and take one step. Third, content is flexible. The programme draws on the screening questionnaire and a needs check-in at the start, and sessions can be adjusted later if predesigned content does not fit the actual group.

## 6. What strategies will be used to evaluate how well the stated objectives of the group are met?

The evaluation plan combines formative and summative approaches.

Formative strategies include pre- and post-programme self-clarity scaling questions (see Appendix A), tracking the progression of each member's Miro canvas across sessions, facilitator observation notes recorded after every session, and a mid-programme informal feedback check after Session 5.

Summative strategies include the Career Adapt-Abilities Scale Short Form (CAAS-SF; Maggiori et al., 2017) administered before Session 1 and after Session 9, each member's reflection and action plan presented in Session 9, individual progress noted during the individual counselling sessions, and a post-programme feedback questionnaire with both Likert-scale and open-ended items.

## 7. What recruitment or selection procedures will be used in forming the group?

Members are recruited through the facilitator's personal network, including alumni connections, less-close classmates, friends-of-friends, and similar contacts. Recruitment messages describe the topic in everyday language, for example, "Not sure what to do after graduation? Stuck between further study and starting work, between staying or going back home? If you would like to explore yourself in a safe small group, here is some information." To avoid dual relationships, the facilitator does not recruit close friends, roommates, or anyone with a power or interest relationship to the facilitator.

Interested individuals will be selected through a short online screening questionnaire covering basic information, open questions about their experience of career uncertainty, what they hope to gain from the group, and exclusion criteria screening (see Appendix B). A brief 10-to-15-minute individual pre-group conversation will confirm fit, explain group norms, and answer questions. The target group size is six members. If more than six suitable applicants respond, selection will prioritise diversity of experience and readiness to engage.

---

## 8. How are the members going to be prepared for the group experience?

Pre-test (baseline data): Members complete the CAAS-SF and self-clarity scaling questions before the programme starts.

Informed consent: Members sign a consent form covering the group's purpose and structure, confidentiality and its limits, recording policy, and the right to withdraw.

Pre-group individual conversation (10 to 15 minutes): Each member speaks briefly with the facilitator to clarify expectations, address concerns, and build initial rapport.

Information sheet: Members receive a one-page document covering the schedule, brief instructions for Zoom and Miro, and the group rules.

Needs check-in during Session 1: "Why are you here? What do you hope to take away from this group?"

## Appendix A: Self-Clarity Scaling Questions

**Instruction:** Please rate according to your current real feelings.

**Rating:** 1 = Completely unaware/unclear → 10 = Very aware/very clear

1. My understanding of my interests (what activities give me energy): ___/10
2. My understanding of my strengths (what I am good at): ___/10
3. My understanding of my values (what is truly important to me): ___/10
4. My clarity on my future direction (how well I know where to go): ___/10
5. My confidence in "what to do next": ___/10

## Appendix B: Online Screening Questionnaire

**Basic information:**

- "What do you want others to call you?"
- "How old are you?"
- "What stage are you currently in? A student? Just graduated? Already in employment?"

**Open questions:**

- "Please briefly describe how you currently feel about your future career or life direction."
- "What do you most hope to gain from this group?"
- "Are you willing to share your ideas in the group and listen to others?"

**Exclusion screening:**

- "Are you currently undergoing psychiatric treatment or in a severe psychological crisis?"
